# Individual Insights: Development Process

> Module 6 · Launch Impactful GTM Plans, ★ Deliverable 6 (reflection)

## Friction points

One of my biggest challenges was distinguishing the symptoms of a problem from its root cause. Early qualitative research focused on drivers, but later quantitative evidence pointed toward dispatch coordinators as the more relevant audience. This required reassessing our original assumptions and refining the product direction. Another challenge was designing an experiment that was statistically sound while remaining feasible within a limited pilot.

## Key learnings

This course taught me to approach product management as a continuous cycle of discovery, prioritization, experimentation, and learning. I learned to distinguish verified evidence from assumptions, use quantitative and qualitative data to challenge product decisions, and prioritize solutions based on user value and implementation effort. Building the RouteLogic prototype demonstrated how AI can accelerate product development, while the experimentation and launch planning exercises reinforced that a working prototype is not proof of business impact. My biggest takeaway is that strong product decisions require measurable outcomes, clear guardrails, and a willingness to adapt when new evidence emerges.

## Aha! moment

My biggest aha moment was realizing that product management is not about defending an initial solution but continuously challenging it with evidence. We started with driver-focused research, but the quantitative data led us to reconsider our target user and focus on dispatch coordinators. I learned that changing direction based on new evidence is a strength, not a failure, and that even a promising solution must be validated before launch.
