Problem Hook & Value Proposition

Problem Hook & Value Proposition, Module 1

  • Scenario: RouteLogic Velocity (B2B)

Strategic crisis

If RouteLogic fails to address its platform's complexity over the next 12 months, it risks losing enterprise customers to simpler competitors, reducing customer retention, weakening its competitive position, and potentially losing revenue.

Moment of misery

Users are forced to spend extra time navigating RouteLogic's complex platform to complete routine tasks, slowing down their daily operations and forcing them to Google, spreadsheets, group texts, etc.

Problem hook

RouteLogic's complex platform makes everyday tasks harder for users, reducing operational efficiency and putting enterprise customer retention at risk as simpler competitors become more attractive.

Value proposition

For Frontline coordinators, we will make RouteLogic easier to use and improve operational efficiency because Enterprise customer retention and competitive position are at risk.

Cold-read self-review

The problem is important because RouteLogic's software complexity affects frontline coordinators and puts enterprise customer retention at risk. Improving usability would address the core user challenge while supporting RouteLogic's business goals.

Data-Backed Hypothesis

Data-Backed Hypothesis, Module 3

  • Scenario: RouteLogic Velocity (B2B)
  • Bet type: Optimizing the existing

The hypothesis

Based on a 48% compliance-check completion rate, an average completion time of 14.6 minutes versus a 3-minute benchmark, and coordinator NPS declining from +18 to -12, we believe that solving the complexity of RouteLogic's compliance-check workflow and the lack of clear guidance through required steps for dispatch coordinators responsible for daily fleet operations and compliance checks will result in higher compliance-check completion rates, improved operational efficiency, and reduced workflow friction that supports enterprise customer retention, as measured by an increase in compliance-check completion from 48% to at least 60% (a 12-percentage-point improvement and 25% relative increase). We will protect compliance-check accuracy at or above its pre-pilot baseline, with no increase in missing information, incorrect entries, or invalid submissions and make a go/no-go decision after a 14-day pilot, using sufficient representative data to determine whether to scale, iterate, or stop based on completion rates and compliance accuracy.

Evidence

  • Qualitative (M2): Module 2 interviews revealed that RouteLogic's complexity forces frontline employees to use manual workarounds. UXR-04 reported that employees use only about 5% of the platform. These findings suggest a broader usability problem, but coordinator-specific interviews are still needed to confirm the causes of compliance-check friction.
  • Quantitative (M3): a 48% compliance-check completion rate, an average completion time of 14.6 minutes versus a 3-minute benchmark, and coordinator NPS declining from +18 to -12

Persona & problem

  • Role: dispatch coordinators responsible for daily fleet operations and compliance checks
  • Goal: Complete daily compliance checks accurately and efficiently while keeping fleet operations on schedule.
  • Friction: Complex, time-consuming compliance workflows slow coordinators down and contribute to low completion rates.
  • Problem you are solving: the complexity of RouteLogic's compliance-check workflow and the lack of clear guidance through required steps

Outcome & metrics

  • Strategic outcome: higher compliance-check completion rates, improved operational efficiency, and reduced workflow friction that supports enterprise customer retention
  • Primary success metric: an increase in compliance-check completion from 48% to at least 60% (a 12-percentage-point improvement and 25% relative increase)
  • Guardrail metric: compliance-check accuracy at or above its pre-pilot baseline, with no increase in missing information, incorrect entries, or invalid submissions
  • Decision window: a 14-day pilot, using sufficient representative data to determine whether to scale, iterate, or stop based on completion rates and compliance accuracy

Experimentation Plan

A/B Experiment Brief, RouteLogic (B2B)

Parameters

Parameter · Decision

Feature under test · Step Progress Indicator for dispatch coordinator compliance checks

Persona · Dispatch coordinators responsible for completing compliance checks and managing daily delivery operations.

Expected outcome · Higher compliance-check completion rates

Primary success metric · Compliance-check completion rate (% of eligible coordinator compliance checks successfully completed).

Baseline rate · 48%

Guardrail metric · Compliance accuracy

Guardrail boundary · No decline relative to control (0 percentage-point decrease allowed); no invalid submissions accepted

Minimum Detectable Effect · 12 percentage points

Sample size per arm · 270

Traffic split · 50/50

Test duration · 14 days

Significance threshold · p < 0.05

Control vs. Variant

  • Control (A): Dispatch coordinators use the existing RouteLogic compliance-check workflow without a Step Progress Indicator. They must navigate the current process without a visual display of completed, current, and remaining steps. Existing required fields, validation rules, and submission behavior remain unchanged.
  • Variant (B): Dispatch coordinators use the existing compliance-check workflow with one added feature: the Step Progress Indicator. It visually displays completed, current, and remaining required steps and updates automatically as each step is completed. All existing required fields, validation rules, and submission behavior remain unchanged.
  • Held constant (isolation check): Both groups use the same RouteLogic application, compliance-check steps, required fields, validation rules, submission process, and completion confirmation. User permissions, notifications, onboarding, and access to existing features remain identical. The only difference is that Variant B displays the Step Progress Indicator.

Hypothesis

I believe adding a Step Progress Indicator to the existing compliance-check workflow for dispatch coordinators will increase compliance-check completion by making completed, current, and remaining steps visible. We aim to improve the completion rate from the observed 48% baseline to at least 60%, a proposed 12-percentage-point increase, during a 14-day experiment. Compliance accuracy must not decline relative to the control group, and invalid submissions must remain blocked.

Shipping Criteria

  • Ship: The variant demonstrates an improvement of at least 12 percentage points versus the control group, with p < 0.05, while compliance accuracy does not decline and no invalid submissions are accepted. Confirm the planned sample size and valid randomization before interpreting the result.
  • Iterate: Results suggest a positive effect, but the lift is below 12 percentage points or evidence is inconclusive, with no safety violation. Investigate what to improve and test again; do not treat this as a confirmed win.
  • Kill: The feature shows no useful improvement or a negative effect after a sufficiently powered, valid experiment. Do not equate a nonsignificant result from an underpowered test with proof of no effect.
  • Investigate / pause: Any compliance guardrail breach, unexpected segment differences, instrumentation problems, contamination, or insufficient independent participants requires review before a shipping decision.
  • Read date: Evaluate the pre-specified results at the end of 14 days, provided the planned sample and data-quality conditions are met. Do not make an early shipping decision based on interim results.

Assumptions to verify before running

  • Metric definition: Confirm the original 48% funnel baseline uses the same denominator and definition as the experiment's completed-check rate.
  • Sample feasibility: The builder estimates 270 independent coordinators per arm (540 total) at 80% power and 5% significance for a 12-percentage-point effect. The three-account pilot has not been shown to provide this many independent participants; account-level clustering or cross-group contamination may require a different design or larger sample.
  • Duration: The 14-day measurement period can fit inside the previously proposed four-week pilot window, but duration alone does not guarantee enough data.
  • Guardrail measurement: Establish an auditable compliance-accuracy measurement and confirm that all existing validation rules remain enforced in both groups.

GTM Launch Plan & Success Dashboard

GTM Launch Plan, RouteLogic (B2B)

Field · Value

Feature · Step Progress Indicator for dispatch coordinator compliance checks

Goal · Engagement

Launch tier · M, Targeted

Goal & Audience

  • Goal: Engagement, RouteLogic already serves dispatch coordinators, so the priority is improving engagement with the existing compliance-check workflow rather than acquiring new users. Module 3 identified a 48% compliance-check completion baseline. The Step Progress Indicator aims to improve workflow clarity and increase completion to a proposed 60%, provided the experiment validates its effectiveness without reducing compliance accuracy.
  • Target audience: Primary audience: Existing RouteLogic dispatch coordinators responsible for completing compliance checks and managing daily delivery operations.

Secondary audience: Customer success leads and operations managers at participating enterprise accounts who support coordinator adoption, monitor compliance performance, and provide feedback during rollout.

Launch Tier

  • M, Targeted, A targeted (M) launch is appropriate because the Step Progress Indicator is a focused workflow improvement for existing RouteLogic customers, not a new product or market expansion. Coordinated email, in-app messaging, and customer success outreach can reach dispatch coordinators without the expense of a broad marketing campaign. The rollout will remain conditional on successful experiment results and compliance safeguards.

Channels

1. Owned: In-app messaging (Owned): Display a contextual announcement and brief walkthrough when dispatch coordinators open the compliance-check workflow, explaining the new Step Progress Indicator and how to use it.

2. Owned: Targeted Email (Owned): Send a focused product update to dispatch coordinators and operations managers at participating RouteLogic accounts. Explain the Step Progress Indicator's benefits, provide a short walkthrough, and direct users to the updated compliance-check workflow.

3. Owned: Customer Success Outreach (Owned): Have RouteLogic customer success leads coordinate short demonstrations and onboarding sessions with operations managers and dispatch coordinators at participating accounts. Collect feedback, address adoption barriers, and reinforce proper use of the Step Progress Indicator.

Enablement & Assets

Prepare a short feature walkthrough, in-app announcement, targeted email template, and one-page quick-start guide for dispatch coordinators. Equip Customer Success with a demonstration script, FAQ, and feedback collection template. Provide Support with troubleshooting guidance and an escalation process. Confirm all materials accurately explain the Step Progress Indicator, required compliance steps, and submission validation rules before launch.

Ownership, Budget & Timeline

  • Ownership & budget: Product Manager: Own launch coordination, go/no-go decision, success metrics, and stakeholder alignment. Engineering (2 engineers): Own feature readiness, instrumentation, and technical support. Product Designer (1 designer): Own in-app messaging and walkthrough assets. Customer Success Lead (1 lead): Own targeted email outreach, account onboarding, demonstrations, and feedback collection. Support: Own troubleshooting documentation and issue escalation.

Budget: No paid advertising planned. Use existing internal resources and customer communication channels. Confirm staff availability, asset production effort, and any incremental costs before launch. Individual owners to be assigned.

  • Timeline: Phase 1: Beta (Weeks 1–2, provisional): Conduct the planned A/B experiment with participating RouteLogic accounts. Before starting, verify that the pilot can provide the required 540 eligible, independently measured coordinators and reliable instrumentation. Run the planned 14-day measurement period only if feasible; otherwise, revise the experiment timeline or recruitment plan before making a launch decision.

Phase 2: Launch (Week 3, conditional): If experiment results meet the predefined shipping criteria, release the Step Progress Indicator to the approved audience. Activate in-app messaging, targeted email communication, and Customer Success onboarding. If results are inconclusive or safeguards fail, delay launch and investigate.

Phase 3: Post-Launch (Week 4 and ongoing): Monitor feature adoption, compliance-check completion, and compliance accuracy. Collect coordinator feedback, resolve issues, and decide whether to expand, iterate, or pause the rollout.

Success Metrics

  • Metrics: 1. Compliance-check completion rate (primary): Percentage of eligible compliance-check workflows started by dispatch coordinators that reach successful, valid submission. Compare the observed 48% baseline with the proposed 60% target (+12 percentage points), using consistent eligibility criteria, event definitions, and denominators. Confirm the historical baseline matches this definition before interpreting experiment results.

2. Feature adoption: Track the percentage of eligible dispatch coordinators who use the Step Progress Indicator after launch. Establish the adoption baseline and target during rollout planning.

3. Compliance-check task time: Reduce median completion time from the observed 14.6 minutes toward the proposed target of 11.7 minutes or less.

Guardrail: Compliance accuracy must not decline relative to the control group, and no invalid submissions may be accepted.

  • Bad signal to watch for: Watch for declining compliance accuracy, invalid submissions, low feature adoption, increased task completion time, or coordinator feedback indicating confusion. A completion-rate improvement accompanied by compliance errors is not a successful outcome. Also monitor insufficient sample size or unreliable experiment data, which would prevent a confident launch decision.
  • Likely post-launch decision: The most likely decision is to iterate based on coordinator feedback and observed adoption, unless the experiment demonstrates a clear reason to expand or pause. Double down if a valid A/B test shows at least a 12-percentage-point improvement in compliance-check completion (p < 0.05) with no decline in compliance accuracy. Iterate if results are positive but below the target or inconclusive. Investigate and delay expansion if sample size or data quality is insufficient. Pause or roll back if compliance safeguards fail. Deprioritize if a sufficiently powered experiment shows no meaningful benefit.
Module 6

Individual Insights

Where the work was hardest, the moment my thinking changed, and what I learned.

Friction points

One of my biggest challenges was distinguishing the symptoms of a problem from its root cause. Early qualitative research focused on drivers, but later quantitative evidence pointed toward dispatch coordinators as the more relevant audience. This required reassessing our original assumptions and refining the product direction. Another challenge was designing an experiment that was statistically sound while remaining feasible within a limited pilot.

Aha moment

My biggest aha moment was realizing that product management is not about defending an initial solution but continuously challenging it with evidence. We started with driver-focused research, but the quantitative data led us to reconsider our target user and focus on dispatch coordinators. I learned that changing direction based on new evidence is a strength, not a failure, and that even a promising solution must be validated before launch.

Key learnings

This course taught me to approach product management as a continuous cycle of discovery, prioritization, experimentation, and learning. I learned to distinguish verified evidence from assumptions, use quantitative and qualitative data to challenge product decisions, and prioritize solutions based on user value and implementation effort. Building the RouteLogic prototype demonstrated how AI can accelerate product development, while the experimentation and launch planning exercises reinforced that a working prototype is not proof of business impact. My biggest takeaway is that strong product decisions require measurable outcomes, clear guardrails, and a willingness to adapt when new evidence emerges.
