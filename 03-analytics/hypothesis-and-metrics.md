## Evidence Reconciliation and Strategic Pivot

Module 2 identified driver workflow complexity and manual workarounds as an initial product opportunity. However, Module 3 revealed stronger quantitative evidence of coordinator workflow friction, including a 48% compliance-check completion rate, substantial task-time inefficiency, and declining coordinator NPS. Based on these findings, the Module 3 hypothesis shifts its focus to dispatch coordinators. The original driver findings remain valid discovery evidence, but coordinator-specific qualitative research is still needed to confirm the root causes of their workflow difficulties.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Module 2 research identified excessive platform complexity and manual workarounds. UXR-04 reported that frontline employees use only about 5% of the platform and struggle to find essential actions, while UXR-01 described bypassing a cumbersome delivery workflow by texting a dispatcher. These findings support a broader usability problem but do not directly establish coordinator-specific friction. Module 3's coordinator findings therefore warrant targeted qualitative validation before scaling a solution.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Module 3 data reveals significant coordinator workflow friction. Only 48% of coordinators complete compliance checks, 31% complete shift handoff, and 18% file daily reports. Compliance checks take 14.6 minutes against a 3-minute benchmark, while coordinator NPS has fallen from +18 to -12 over two years. These metrics demonstrate substantial workflow inefficiency and declining satisfaction.d
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Dispatch coordinators responsible for assigning routes, completing compliance checks, managing shift handoffs, and filing daily reports. Their goal is to coordinate daily fleet operations accurately and efficiently. Complex, time-consuming workflows create bottlenecks, particularly during compliance checks, contributing to low workflow completion rates and operational inefficiency.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Simplify RouteLogic's coordinator compliance-check workflow by eliminating unnecessary steps and navigation that slow daily operations, contribute to workflow abandonment, and delay completion of subsequent tasks.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Increase the percentage of dispatch coordinators who complete compliance checks and subsequent daily workflow tasks directly in RouteLogic by reducing unnecessary effort and time spent. Improved workflow completion and operational efficiency should increase coordinator satisfaction, reduce reliance on manual workarounds, and help lower enterprise customer churn risk, protecting recurring revenue.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Increase the coordinator compliance-check completion rate from its current 48% baseline to at least 60% during the 14-day pilot, representing a 12-percentage-point improvement (25% relative increase).

Simplifying the workflow must not increase missing compliance information, incorrect entries, or incomplete required checks.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Conduct a 14-day pilot of the simplified coordinator compliance-check workflow. Scale if completion increases from 48% to at least 60%, with sufficient representative data and no decline in compliance accuracy. If improvement falls below 60%, investigate the remaining friction and iterate or extend testing. Stop or roll back if compliance accuracy deteriorates.
